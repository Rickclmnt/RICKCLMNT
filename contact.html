Licensing arrangements.

Terms are negotiated according to the technology, intended application, scope and commercial requirements.

TERMS

Non-exclusive license

Permission to use the technology without exclusive rights. Other parties may receive compatible licenses.

Exclusive license

Exclusive usage or commercialization rights within a negotiated field, territory or market.

Commercial license

Permission to integrate and use the technology in commercial products or services.

OEM / Integration

Rights to incorporate the technology into another organization's products or systems.

Enterprise license

Organizational usage rights with negotiated deployment and operational scope.

Custom arrangement

Bespoke licensing structures, partnerships, sublicensing or other negotiated rights.

OWNERSHIP
A license does not automatically transfer ownership. Intellectual property ownership remains with its owner unless expressly transferred through a separate agreement.

Frequently asked questions.

    


    

      
FAQ

      

        

          
Who owns the developed technologies?

          
Ownership is retained by the respective intellectual property owner unless a separate written agreement explicitly transfers ownership.

        

        

          
Can I license technology for commercial use?

          
Yes. Commercial usage, integration and distribution rights can be discussed through an appropriate licensing arrangement.

        

        

          
Can I commission completely new technology?

          
You can submit a custom development or research proposal for technical and commercial evaluation.

        

        

          
Are licensing prices fixed?

          
No. Pricing depends on the technology, rights, deployment scale, territory, exclusivity and commercial scope.

        

        

          
Can I acquire an entire technology?

          
Acquisition proposals can be submitted for consideration. Any transfer of ownership or intellectual property rights requires a separate agreement.

        

        

          
Does submitting an inquiry create a contract?

          
No. An inquiry is a request for consideration. A binding relationship requires agreed terms and execution of the appropriate contract.
