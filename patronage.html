Why Patronage Exists

Support the researcher, not a pitch deck.

RICKCLMNT is independent. That independence matters to me.

It means I can investigate ideas because I believe they are worth investigating rather than because a board, employer, investor, or customer has already decided that they are commercially safe.

But independence requires resources. Computers cost money. Components cost money. Materials cost money. Experiments cost money. Most importantly, research requires time.

Patronage provides the resources and runway that allow me to spend more of that time researching, experimenting, and building.

Patronage is intentionally different from conventional investment. You are not buying shares in RICKCLMNT, you are not buying ownership of every project, and you are not being promised a financial return. You are supporting the work because you want to see it happen.

What Patrons Actually Get

Proximity.

The most valuable thing patronage provides isn't a badge. It is proximity to work that is still being created.

You get to see ideas move from:

Question → Experiment → Prototype → Technology

before the outcome is obvious.

A patron might see a project when it is still a sketch. Then an architecture. Then a strange prototype on a workbench. Then something that actually works.

That's the relationship I want to build.

Founding Patrons

Be here before the result.

The first people to support an idea rarely know whether it will succeed. That's what makes early support meaningful.

Founding Patrons are the people who choose to support RICKCLMNT while the laboratory is still being built. Founding Patron status recognizes that contribution.

It isn't an investment contract. It isn't a promise of financial return. It is a record that you were here when the work was still uncertain.

Project Sponsorship

Have a project you want to see investigated?

What sponsorship can support

Equipment, materials, computing hardware, prototype construction, software development, testing, research time, specialist services, and experimental runs.

What sponsorship means

Project sponsorship can help make a particular experiment or prototype possible. It does not mean that the sponsor automatically owns the resulting technology.

What remains separate

Ownership, licensing, exclusivity, and acquisition are negotiated separately where appropriate. See Licensing →
