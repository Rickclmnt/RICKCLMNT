20N

Programmable Neuron Architecture.

20N is a programmable neural architecture in which individual neurons can be assigned explicit functional instructions while retaining the capacity to adapt through training. It explores a different way to construct neural systems: combining programmed behaviour, adaptive learning, and traceable functional organization.

01 / OVERVIEW

Rethinking neural architecture.

Conventional neural networks generally rely on learned parameters to determine how information is processed. Their behaviour emerges through training rather than being explicitly assigned to each individual computational unit.

20N explores a different arrangement. Individual neurons can be given defined functional instructions, establishing what they are intended to do within a larger network.

These instructions provide an explicit structural foundation. Training can then improve how the network performs its assigned functions, allowing adaptation without making the original design entirely implicit.

The objective is to combine the flexibility of adaptive systems with the clarity and control of programmable architecture.

02 / ARCHITECTURE

Programmable by design.

A 20N network can be organized around functional responsibilities. Neurons may receive instructions governing how they process information, what conditions they respond to, and what outputs they produce.

Multiple neurons can work together to form more complex operations. Their individual functions contribute to the behaviour of the overall network.

03 / DEMONSTRATIONS

20N in action.

These two projects explore how a programmable neural architecture can be applied to physical systems. Each demonstration focuses on a different control problem.

DEMO 01 AERIAL ROBOTICS

Quadcopter Control

Applying 20N to the control of a quadcopter. This project explores how programmable neurons can process flight-related inputs and contribute to the control decisions required for stable, responsive movement.

The network is organized around functional responsibilities such as interpreting sensor information, evaluating flight conditions, and producing control outputs.

04 / APPLICATIONS

Beyond the demonstrations.

Quadcopter control and robotic manipulation provide two different environments for exploring the architecture. However, 20N is not limited to robotics.

Its programmable approach can be investigated across other computational systems in which individual functional units need to perform defined tasks while contributing to a larger adaptive system.

Potential areas of exploration include intelligent software, autonomous systems, simulation, decision systems, and other applications of neural computation.
