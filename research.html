Research Areas

Areas of exploration

RICKCLMNT is deliberately interdisciplinary.

Problems don't always respect the boundaries between fields, so neither does the laboratory.

01

AI & Cognitive Systems

Systems for reasoning, memory, perception, decision-making, knowledge representation, and machine cognition.

Projects include Aether, an attempt to build a deterministic cognitive system that can reason from explicit knowledge rather than simply generate plausible answers.

02

Materials & Energy

New approaches to energy generation, storage, structural materials, and material systems.

The objective isn't to make another theoretical material on paper. It is to determine whether an architecture can actually work.

03

Robotics & Human Augmentation

Machines that interact with the physical world and technologies that extend human capability.

This includes robotic systems, wearable machines, assistive technologies, and human-machine interfaces.

04

Software & Computing

Software architectures designed around reliability, autonomy, local execution, inspectability, and unconventional approaches to computation.

05

Experimental Systems

Projects that don't yet have a category.

Some ideas are too early to know what field they belong to. That's fine. They can be investigated anyway.

Current Questions

What's open right now.

Every project in the archive started as a question the laboratory hadn't answered yet. These are the ones currently under active investigation.

Can a cognitive system reason reliably from explicit, inspectable knowledge instead of statistical pattern-matching — and still generalize?

This is the question behind Aether: whether determinism and adaptability are actually in conflict, or whether that's just an assumption inherited from the current generation of AI systems.

Can a structural material also be a power system, without compromising either function?

Energy Brick is testing whether load-bearing composite structures can carry meaningful energy storage and generation without becoming worse at either job.

Where does deterministic processing actually help — and where does it just add friction?

ALIS is the test case for this in a legal context, where auditability and explicit rules may matter more than raw capability.
