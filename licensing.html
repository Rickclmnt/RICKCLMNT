A successful project doesn't have to remain inside the archive forever. When a technology becomes mature enough, it may be made available through licensing, partnership, deployment, or acquisition.

Technology Transfer

Paths to access.

01

Research Access

Access to technical information, demonstrations, or research materials.

02

Non-Exclusive Licensing

Permission to use the technology under agreed terms while allowing it to be licensed elsewhere.

03

Exclusive Licensing

Exclusive rights within an agreed field, territory, market, or application.

04

Acquisition

A complete sale of a technology or its associated intellectual property.

Every project is different. Terms are negotiated individually.

Patronage and technology transactions remain separate.

Supporting the laboratory does not automatically give a patron ownership of its inventions.

Ownership

The work remains independent.

Unless a separate agreement says otherwise, the intellectual property developed through RICKCLMNT remains under my ownership.

Patronage provides support. It does not silently transfer ownership.

If a patron, company, or institution wants commercial rights to a technology, that becomes a separate conversation.

It allows the research to remain independent while still giving successful technologies a path into the real world.
