The Lab

A laboratory built around questions.

It starts with one researcher, a problem that seems worth solving, and the willingness to spend the time necessary to investigate it.

I don't want to build things simply because there is an obvious market for them. I want to investigate things because they are interesting, difficult, useful, or potentially important.

Research begins with a question. Engineering turns the question into something testable. Experiments determine what survives contact with reality. Working technology is what remains.

Patronage

Help make the research possible.

RICKCLMNT is independent. That independence matters to me.

It means I can investigate ideas because I believe they are worth investigating rather than because a board, employer, investor, or customer has already decided that they are commercially safe. But independence requires resources — computers, components, materials, experiments, and most of all, time.

Patronage provides the resources and runway that allow me to spend more of that time researching, experimenting, and building.

Technology Transfer

When research becomes technology.

A successful project doesn't have to remain inside the archive forever.

When a technology becomes mature enough, it may be made available through research access, non-exclusive or exclusive licensing, or full acquisition. Every project is different — terms are negotiated individually.

Patronage and technology transactions remain separate. Supporting the laboratory does not automatically give a patron ownership of its inventions.
