Why RICKCLMNT Exists

Build what is worth building.

I'm a software engineer and independent researcher interested in building things that don't already have obvious answers.

RICKCLMNT is where I put that curiosity to work.

I don't want to spend my life only implementing other people's ideas. I want to investigate my own. RICKCLMNT exists to make that possible.

Philosophy / Independence

No manufactured institution.

RICKCLMNT is currently small. Very small.

So far, it's just me.

I don't want the website to pretend otherwise. There is no artificial team page filled with imaginary departments. No inflated headcount. No manufactured corporate structure. The laboratory is a researcher-led operation.

As the work grows, collaborators, specialists, manufacturers, institutions, and commercial partners may become part of individual projects.

But the underlying principle remains: the work should speak for itself.

My interests move across disciplines because many of the problems I find interesting sit between them. Artificial intelligence leads into cognition. Cognition leads into neuroscience and philosophy. Energy leads into materials. Materials lead into manufacturing. Robotics leads into mechanics, software, control systems, and human capability.

I don't know which projects will become important. Nobody does at the beginning. That's the nature of research. The job is to investigate honestly, build carefully, test aggressively, and follow the evidence.

Some ideas will die. Some will become useful. A few may become technologies that matter.

I want to find out which ones.

Current State of the Laboratory

Where things stand.

RICKCLMNT is a solo, researcher-led operation working across four active projects — Aether, ALIS, ATLAS, and Energy Brick — plus several ideas still too early for the public archive.

There is no team, no office, no funding round. The laboratory runs on independent research time and, where patrons choose to support it, patronage.

Background

Patrick Clement

I'm a software engineer, systems architect, and independent researcher based in Nigeria. I design, build, and explore ideas at the intersection of artificial intelligence, cognition, and human potential.

I'm an engineer by discipline, but a researcher by temperament. Programming is the medium I use to explore deeper questions: how intelligence works, how systems emerge, and how we can build technology that is not only powerful, but understandable.

My path into computer science has been unconventional. I didn't follow a traditional academic route; instead, I built my education through curiosity, independent study, experimentation, and years of self-directed practical work. My background spans computer science, AI, cybersecurity, cognitive systems, and engineering, with courses drawn from wherever I found the strongest material. It's an unusual mix, but that breadth has become one of my strengths.
